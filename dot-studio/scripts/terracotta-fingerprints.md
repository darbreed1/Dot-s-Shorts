# Who built the Terracotta Army?

- **Content type:** Current affairs (history / archaeology)
- **Why now:** Fingerprint study in the *Journal of Cultural Heritage*, covered widely 27–29 October 2026.
- **Idea:** Fingerprints left in the clay of the 2,200-year-old Terracotta Army suggest women and children under 15 helped make the warriors, not only men, though the sample is small and from one pit.
- **Word count:** 66

## Voiceover

Who built China's Terracotta Army? Not just men. The clay soldiers are about 2,200 years old, and their makers left fingerprints in the clay. Smaller hands, like women's and children's, have tighter-packed ridges. Researchers measured 79 prints from one pit. About a third came from women, and a fifth from children under 15. It's a small sample, but this army had more helpers than we thought.

## Notes for production

- Shares are of fingerprints, not a headcount of workers (one person can leave several prints). Reported split: ~45% adult men, ~32.7% adult women, ~22.3% under 15.
- Prints come from 79 fingerprints in excavation unit T23 of Pit 1. The method compares ridge density with modern reference data, which the authors flag as a limitation.
