### Short 3 (publish first): Grace Hopper's nanosecond
Title: Grace Hopper Carried a Nanosecond in Her Pocket ⏱️
Description:
Grace Hopper, computer pioneer and US Navy admiral, handed out pieces of wire about 30 cm long: the farthest light can travel in a nanosecond. A microsecond was a coil 300 m long. Cut her wire into three and you get one tick of a 3 GHz processor: light only gets about 10 cm, roughly the width of your hand.

Sources: https://highscalability.com/grace-hopper-to-programmers-mind-your-nanoseconds/ · https://computerhope.com/jargon/n/nanosec.htm

### Short 1 (second): What does a computer actually do?
Title: Your Computer Can Only Do One Thing 💡
Description:
Every photo, song and game on your computer comes down to one trick. Inside the chip are billions of transistors, each holding a single yes or no. Eight in a row can stand for any of 256 things, a letter, a colour or a number. And all your computer can really do is tell whether a switch is on, or off.

### Short 5 (third): Why does your computer need a fan?
Title: Why Does Your Computer Need a Fan? 🌀
Description:
The heat comes from billions of tiny switches flipping on and off, up to billions of times a second. Each flip takes a tiny bit of electricity, and almost all of it ends up as heat. The fan, fins and pipes carry it away before the chip has to slow itself down. When your fan spins up, that's the sound of arithmetic.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processor_power_dissipation

### Short 2 (fourth): How many switches are in a chip?
Title: Counting the Switches in a Chip Would Take 4,000 Years 🔢
Description:
The first chip you could buy, Intel's 4004 from 1971, had about 2,300 switches: count one a second and you'd finish in under an hour. A billion would take over thirty years. Apple's M2 Ultra from 2023 has 134 billion, about 58 million times more. Counting those would take more than 4,000 years.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor_count

### Short 4 (optional, fifth): Why your processor waits
Title: Your Processor Spends a Lot of Time Doing Nothing ⏳
Description:
A processor can finish a step in a fraction of a nanosecond, so fast that the rest of the computer often can't keep up. The data it needs usually sits in memory, and fetching it takes time. So engineers keep small caches right next to the processor, and have it guess what it will need next and start early. A lot of what makes a chip fast is simply waiting less.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU_cache

### Short 6 (not in the publish plan; long-form only unless wanted): Does your computer understand anything?
Title: Your Computer Has Never Seen a Photo 🖼️
Description:
When you open a photo, the chip isn't looking at a face. It's moving numbers that say how bright each tiny dot on your screen should be. The switches don't know what a face, a song or a joke is. They just flip. All the meaning is on your side of the screen.
