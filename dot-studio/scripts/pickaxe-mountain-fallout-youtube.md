## Title
Would Bombing Pickaxe Mountain Cause Nuclear Fallout? ☢️⛰️

## Description
If the US bombs Iran's Pickaxe Mountain, would nuclear fallout spread? The physics says a strike with ordinary bombs can't cause it.

Fallout is the radioactive dust from a nuclear explosion. A nuclear bomb works by squeezing its uranium together perfectly, fast enough for a chain reaction to run away. An ordinary blast does the opposite: it scatters the material. So bombing uranium, or the machines that enrich it, can't set off a nuclear explosion.

The bigger worry at an enrichment site is the gas used to enrich uranium, uranium hexafluoride. In damp air it reacts to form hydrofluoric acid, which is dangerous up close. But it's a heavy gas, and under rock it wouldn't travel far, so experts expect any harm to stay near the site. After the June 2025 strikes on Natanz, Fordow and Isfahan, the UN's nuclear watchdog, the IAEA, reported no rise in radiation outside them.

Pickaxe Mountain (Kuh-e Kolang Gaz La) sits near Natanz, about 100 m underground. Iran denies any nuclear activity there, and it isn't publicly known whether it holds uranium. Only a nuclear weapon would make fallout. A claim that one could be used was dismissed by the White House, and experts call it highly unlikely.
