## Title
A New Dwarf Planet Past Pluto? 🪐🔭

## Description
Is there a new dwarf planet past Pluto? Maybe.

The new Vera C. Rubin Observatory in Chile has spotted its first dwarf planet candidate, called 2026 ED138. It orbits the Sun about 46 times farther out than Earth, beyond Pluto, and early estimates put it somewhere between 600 and 1,500 kilometres across. For comparison, Pluto is about 2,377 km wide.

Why did nobody find it before? It sits in the southern sky, in front of the crowded Milky Way, where one slow-moving dot is easy to miss among millions of stars.

To count as a dwarf planet, an object needs enough mass to pull itself into a round shape. That hasn't been measured yet, so for now 2026 ED138 is still a candidate.
