# Would bombing Pickaxe Mountain cause nuclear fallout?

- **Content type:** Trending (science, physics). Subject category: science / current affairs.
- **Why now:** Pickaxe Mountain (Kuh-e Kolang Gaz La, near Natanz) is back in the news. On 2 November 2026 Tucker
  Carlson claimed on X that a nuclear strike on it could be days away, and the White House dismissed the claim. On
  2-3 November Trump said the US "might have to go after" the site if there is no deal. Many people are asking
  whether a strike would spread nuclear fallout.
- **Idea:** Fallout comes from a nuclear explosion, and bombing uranium can't set one off. The real hazard at an
  enrichment site is a heavy gas that turns to acid in damp air, and it stays close to the site.
- **Word count:** 73 (31.5 s voice, 32.1 s Short)
- **Status:** Approved and produced 2026-11-03 (Pickaxe, out/pickaxe-mountain-fallout-4K.mp4)

## Voiceover

If the US bombs Iran's Pickaxe Mountain, would nuclear fallout spread? Fallout comes from a nuclear explosion, and bombing uranium can't start one. A nuclear bomb squeezes its uranium together perfectly. An ordinary blast scatters it. The real worry is the enrichment gas, which turns to acid in damp air. But it's heavy, and under rock it wouldn't get far. Only a nuclear weapon would make fallout, and experts call that highly unlikely.

## Notes

- This is a live conflict, so the Short sticks to physics and takes no side. No explosions shown hitting people, no
  real people drawn (no politicians), no flags being bombed. The visuals are a mountain cross-section, a uranium
  "puzzle" that has to be squeezed together and an ordinary blast scattering it, a heavy gas cloud that stays low and
  turns into acid drops, and a fallout cloud that is crossed out.
- "Enrichment gas" is uranium hexafluoride (UF6). With moisture it forms hydrofluoric acid. Uranium is more
  chemically than radiologically toxic, and the hazard is local.
- Iran denies any nuclear activity at the site. Reports say centrifuges may have been moved there, and it is not
  known whether it holds uranium. The script makes a general point about bombing uranium, not a claim about what is
  inside.
- "Experts call that highly unlikely" quotes Hans Kristensen (Federation of American Scientists) in TWZ, 2 Nov 2026.
  Trump has also said nuclear weapons aren't needed.
- Supporting evidence, left out for length: after the June 2025 strikes on Natanz, Fordow and Isfahan, the IAEA
  reported no increase in radiation outside the sites. Inside Natanz the main concern was chemical toxicity.
