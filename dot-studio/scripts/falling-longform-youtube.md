On the record chapter: Vesna Vulović was the only survivor of JAT Flight 367 in January 1972, in which 27 people died. Guinness World Records lists her fall as the highest survived without a parachute (10,160 m). A 2009 journalistic investigation argued the plane broke up much lower; one of the journalists conceded the evidence was only circumstantial, and it has never been proved.

On the speed record: the FAI's ratified record is Sebastián García Gutiérrez's 536.75 km/h competition average from August 2025 (his fastest single round there was 539.51 km/h). He went faster at the October 2026 world championships; those records are pending FAI ratification.

### Short 1: How fast do you fall?
Title: How Fast Do You Actually Fall? 🪂
Description:
Jump out of a plane and you stop speeding up after about 12 seconds: that's terminal velocity, about 200 km/h belly-down and 240-290 km/h head-down. The official speed-skydiving record is a 537 km/h average (FAI, August 2025).

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_skydiving · https://www.fai.org/record/20942

### Short 2: The record that might not be true
Title: The Highest Fall Ever Survived Might Not Be True ❓
Description:
Guinness lists Vesna Vulović's 10,160 m fall in January 1972, when she was the only survivor of a plane that broke apart over Czechoslovakia. In 2009, journalists argued it broke up much lower, but admitted the evidence was only circumstantial. It has never been proved either way.

### Short 3: How a parachute works
Title: How a Parachute Works, and What Happens When It Fails 🪂
Description:
Pilot chute, pin, bag, lines, slider, canopy: a parachute opens in a set order. About 1 jump in 1,000 has a malfunction, so every skydiver carries a reserve, repacked every 180 days in the US, and many rigs have an automatic opener as a last backup.

Sources: https://uspa.org/Discover/FAQs/Equipment · https://skydivemidwest.com/what-if-my-parachute-doesnt-open/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_activation_device

### Short 4: Why the space station is falling
Title: The Space Station Is Falling Right Now 🛰️
Description:
At 400 km up, gravity is still about 90% as strong as on the ground. The ISS doesn't hit us because it moves sideways at about 8 km every second: it keeps falling and keeps missing. Around 2030, NASA plans to steer it into a remote part of the ocean.
