## Title
Who Really Built the Terracotta Army? 🏺👣

## Description
Who built China's Terracotta Army? Not just men.

The clay soldiers guarding the tomb of China's first emperor are about 2,200 years old, and the people who made them left fingerprints in the clay. Fingerprint ridges are packed more tightly on smaller hands, like women's and children's, so researchers used ridge density to estimate who left each print.

They measured 79 fingerprints from one excavation unit in Pit 1. About a third came from adult women, about a fifth from children under 15, and the rest from adult men. These are shares of fingerprints, not a headcount of workers (one person can leave several prints), and it's a small sample from one pit, so the researchers are careful about how far it goes. Still, it suggests the army had more helpers than we thought.

Study: "Who produced Emperor Qin Shihuang's Terracotta Army, China? A Bayesian mixture model approach to fingerprint age and sex determination," Journal of Cultural Heritage (2026): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1296207426002062
