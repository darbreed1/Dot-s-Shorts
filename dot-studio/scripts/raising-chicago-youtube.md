# YouTube upload text: Why did Chicago lift its buildings?

## Title
Chicago Lifted Whole Buildings With People Inside 🏙️⬆️

## Description
In the 1850s and 60s, Chicago lifted whole buildings, sometimes while people were still inside.

The city was built on low, swampy ground, barely above Lake Michigan. Water sat in the streets, and sewers need a downhill slope to drain, but there wasn't one. So, under engineer Ellis Chesbrough's sewer plan (1856), the city raised its streets by several feet, laying the sewers and covering them with fill. Buildings had to rise to match.

Crews did it with jackscrews. In 1860, about 600 men turning 6,000 jackscrews lifted a whole row of Lake Street shops, roughly 4 feet 8 inches, in five days, and the businesses stayed open the whole time. A year later the six-storey Tremont House hotel was raised about 6 feet with guests still staying there.

(The Lake Street details come from 1860 Chicago newspaper reports, as quoted in the sources below.)
