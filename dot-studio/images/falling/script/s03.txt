What's the highest fall anyone has ever survived without a parachute? According to Guinness World Records, it's 10,160 metres. In January 1972, a Yugoslav airliner broke apart in the sky over what was then Czechoslovakia. The official investigation blamed a bomb. Twenty-eight people were on board, and twenty-seven of them died. The one survivor was a 22-year-old flight attendant called Vesna Vulović. A man from a nearby village found her trapped in the wreckage, badly injured. She was in a coma for three days, and it took her months to walk again. But she lived. And her fall, from the plane's cruising height, went into the record books. Then, in 2009, two journalists challenged that number. They argued the plane actually broke up much lower, maybe only a few hundred metres up, after a fast emergency descent. They pointed to how small an area the wreckage covered, and to people on the ground who saw it come down. They even suggested the plane might have been shot down by mistake, and that the high-altitude bomb story was invented to hide it. But one of the journalists admitted the evidence was only circumstantial, and it has never been proved. Guinness still lists the record. So was it 10 kilometres, or a few hundred metres? Either way, she survived something almost nobody survives. We just may never know how far she really fell.
